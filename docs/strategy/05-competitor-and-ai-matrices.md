**Read-out:**
1. AI research is crowded and comes with a steep premium. The only cheap entrant is Prism, at ₹5k/yr.
2. Practice management for solos is either enterprise-priced and demo-gated (Provakil, MyKase) or a hobbyist diary app with no AI.
3. Nobody advertises matter-scoped, lawyer-specific memory at the low end.
4. Every serious vendor now markets verifiability. The 2026 Supreme Court rulings make that table stakes.

## B. AI feature opportunity matrix

Scoring 1–5, where higher is better for us.
- **User value**: frequency × pain relief for a Gen Z litigator.
- **Differentiation**: how far it sits from what incumbents and free ChatGPT already do.
- **Feasibility**: buildable by 1–3 people.
- **Cost-safety**: unit cost compared with a ₹200–500 price point.
- **Risk-safety**: hallucination, confidentiality and professional-rules exposure; 5 means low risk.

Priority = sum of the five scores (max 25). [INFERENCE throughout. Validate the user-value scores in interviews.]

### B2. Why incumbents adopted AI, and what that means for us [INFERENCE]
- **Defending the content moat.** Free or cheap generative search (ChatGPT, Prism) threatens the headnote-subscription business, so SCC, Manupatra and CaseMine bolt "anchored-in-our-database" AI on top and price it as a premium add-on.
- **Upsell lever.** Western practice-management vendors gate AI to higher tiers (MyCase Pro+, Clio higher tiers). Indian research vendors use credit caps (CaseMine 250/500, Manupatra 5 summaries).
- **Enterprise land-grab.** Harvey, Legora and Lucio target tier-1 Indian firms through content partnerships.
- **Our opening:** AI that is *cheap because it is narrow*. It works on the lawyer's own orders, notes and templates, not on a research corpus. Small models handle most tasks, and verification is part of the design.
