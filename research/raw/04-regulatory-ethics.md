## 0. Corrections to the brief (important)

1. **Rule 36 is not in "Section II".** In the BCI Rules text I read (Part VI, Ch. II), Section II is "Duty to the Client" (Rules 11-32). Rule 36 sits in **Section IV, "Duty to Colleagues"**. Rule 37 (unauthorised practice) and Rule 38 (fee not less than taxable fee) are also there. The Advocates Act authority for these rules is s.49(1)(c). [VERIFIED-PRIMARY, with the copy caveat below]
2. **M.V. Dabholkar was decided in 1975**, not 1976. The Supreme Court judgment is dated 3 October 1975 (reported as AIR 1976 SC 242, which explains the "1976" in common use; the report year was not checked here). [VERIFIED-PRIMARY for the date, https://indiankanoon.org/doc/276363/]
3. **The BCI Rules have no express "no fee-sharing with non-lawyers" rule that I could find.** In the text I read, the closest provisions are: Rule 20 (no contingent fee or sharing of litigation proceeds), Rule 37 (no lending name/services to aid unauthorised practice), Rule 24 (no abuse of client confidence), and Rule 36 (no indirect solicitation "by... touts"). Commentary mentions a fee-division rule only on unofficial exam-prep sites (one cites "Rule 29"), which does not match the text I read. Do not cite a specific fee-sharing rule number until counsel confirms. [VERIFIED-PRIMARY for absence in this copy; REVIEW]
4. **The BCI Rules copy I read may be outdated.** It is the Bar Council of Gujarat's PDF of "B.C.I. Rules" and does not show the 2008 website proviso to Rule 36 or a Schedule. The current official consolidated text from barcouncilofindia.org should be pulled by counsel. [REVIEW]
5. **No gazetted BCI AI rule found.** Reports of "BCI 2026 AI guidelines" (e.g. a lawsathi.in page) cite no official BCI document. I treat them as unreliable. [VERIFIED-SECONDARY (absence)]

## 1. Advertising and solicitation by advocates

### 1.1 Statutory frame

- **Advocates Act 1961 s.35** (professional or other misconduct): State Bar Council disciplinary committees can reprimand, suspend, or remove advocates. **s.49(1)(c)** empowers BCI to make rules on standards of professional conduct and etiquette. The BCI Rules Part VI Ch. II are expressly made "under Section 49(1)(c)". [Rules text: VERIFIED-PRIMARY; contents of ss.35/49 beyond the rule header: INFERENCE from general knowledge, not re-read on indiacode this session; REVIEW]
- Source for the Rules text: Bar Council of Gujarat PDF, https://www.barcouncilofgujarat.org/images/ActAndRule/BCI&BCG_RULES_1.PDF (accessed 2026-10-17).

### 1.2 Rule 36 text (as read)

Verbatim from the PDF (OCR artefacts removed): "An Advocate shall not solicit work or advertise, either directly or indirectly, whether by circulars, advertisements, touts, personal communications, interview not warranted by personal relations, furnishing or inspiring newspaper comments or producing his photograph to be published in connection with cases in which he has been engaged or concerned. His sign board or name plate should be of a reasonable size. The sign board or name plate or stationery should not indicate that he is or has been President or Member of a Bar Council or of any Association or that he has been associated with any person or organisation or with any particular cause or matter or that he specialises in any particular type of work or that he has been a Judge or an Advocate General." [VERIFIED-PRIMARY]

Related rules in the same chapter [VERIFIED-PRIMARY]:
- Rule 17: no breach, directly or indirectly, of obligations under "Section 126 of the Indian Evidence Act" (now to be read with the BSA 2023 equivalents; see section 2).
- Rule 18: no fomenting of litigation.
- Rule 19: act only on instructions of client or authorised agent.
- Rule 20: no fee contingent on result, no sharing of proceeds.
- Rule 24: no abuse of client confidence.
- Rules 25-31: client money accounting (relevant to billing, see section 5).
- Rule 37: no permitting services or name "to be used in aid of or to make possible, the unauthorised practise of law by any agency".

### 1.3 The 2008 website proviso

- The BCI resolved on 30 April 2008 to add a proviso to Rule 36 allowing advocates to furnish website information per a Schedule approved by BCI. Any additional content beyond the Schedule is treated as a Rule 36 violation, exposing the advocate to s.35 misconduct proceedings. The resolution was reported as not yet gazetted at the time. Context: V.B. Joshi v. Union of India (SC, 2008) challenging Rule 36. [VERIFIED-SECONDARY: https://itatonline.org/info/advertisement-by-advocates/ ; https://itatonline.org/info/?p=57]
- Schedule contents (as reported): name, address, phone, email; enrolment number and date; State Bar Council(s); bar association memberships; professional and academic qualifications; areas of practice. [VERIFIED-SECONDARY: law-firm commentary summarised in search results; the Schedule itself was NOT located. REVIEW: counsel should obtain the official Schedule and confirm gazette status.]
- Note that the Schedule reportedly includes "areas of practice", yet Rule 36 text still bars signboards/stationery indicating specialisation. Tension to be resolved by counsel.

### 1.4 Case law

- **Bar Council of Maharashtra v. M.V. Dabholkar**, SC, 3 Oct 1975 (Krishna Iyer J.; Sarkaria, Gupta, Fazal Ali JJ.). Held: canons "totally taboo conduct by way of soliciting, advertising, scrambling and other obnoxious practices"; "Law is no trade, briefs no merchandise"; Rule 36 "fairly construed, sets out wholesome rules of professional conduct"; rejected a narrow three-element reading of the rule. Also: ethical norms exist independently of Rule 36. [VERIFIED-PRIMARY: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/276363/ (accessed 2026-10-17)]
- **V.C. Rangadurai v. D. Gopalan** (SC, 1978, on advocate professional ethics and standards, not specifically advertising): not read this session; cite only after counsel pulls the judgment. [INFERENCE; not verified]
- **V.B. Joshi v. Union of India** (SC, 2008): background to the website proviso (see 1.3). [VERIFIED-SECONDARY]
- Other Indian Kanoon hits on Rule 36 (not read): Pandurang Dattatraya Khandekar v. BCM (1983), https://indiankanoon.org/doc/752761/ ; B. Rajeswar Reddy v. K. Narasimhachari (2001), https://indiankanoon.org/doc/311395/ . [Listed only]

### 1.6 Fee-sharing, referral fees, intermediaries

- No express BCI rule on referral commissions to non-lawyers located (see section 0, item 3). Likely hooks: Rule 36 ("touts", indirect solicitation), Rule 37 (aiding unauthorised practice), Rule 18 (fomenting litigation), Rule 20 (no sharing proceeds), s.35 general misconduct, and Advocates Act provisions on who may practise (ss.29, 33; recalled, not re-read). [INFERENCE; REVIEW]
- Pay-per-lead or lead-auction marketplaces and referral-fee revenue shares with non-advocates are the highest-risk structure. [INFERENCE]

### 1.7 Foreign law firms (brief)

- BCI Rules for Registration and Regulation of Foreign Lawyers and Foreign Law Firms in India 2022 (notified March 2023), amended/replaced by 2025 rules gazetted around 13 May 2025: foreign lawyers may advise on foreign/international law in non-litigious matters on a reciprocity basis; Indian law practice and litigation remain barred. [VERIFIED-SECONDARY: https://www.barandbench.com/law-firms/view-point/cross-border-counsel-decoding-the-2025-amendment-to-bci-rules-on-practice-by-foreign-law-firms-and-lawyers-in-india ; https://www.moneylife.in/article/bar-council-allows-entry-of-foreign-lawyers-and-law-firms-notifies-amended-rules/77132.html]

### Product implications for section 1 (all INFERENCE; REVIEW)
- The CRM must not run outreach to prospective clients on behalf of an advocate (cold email/WhatsApp/SMS campaigns, ad targeting, lead-gen funnels). Rule 36 bars "circulars" and "personal communications... not warranted by personal relations".
- "Lawyer profile page" features must be limited to the Schedule fields, be advocate-initiated and advocate-controlled, and ideally run on the advocate's own domain, because the legality of third-party aggregation is pending in the SC.
- Do not build a public directory, ranking, "top lawyer" badges, reviews/testimonials, or client-case showcase pages.
- Do not build content-calendar, SEO-for-lawyers, influencer, or celebrity-endorsement tooling aimed at attracting clients. Educational content for peers or general legal literacy is a grey area (see table).
- No referral fees, commission splits, or paid lead routing between the platform and advocates.
- CRM of existing/past clients and professional contacts (matter management, reminders, intake of inbound enquiries) is the safer core. Inbound enquiry handling must not look like a sales funnel.

## 2. Confidentiality and privilege

- **BCI Rule 17** (Section II): no direct or indirect breach of obligations under IEA s.126. **Rule 24**: no abuse of client's confidence. [VERIFIED-PRIMARY]
- **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam 2023** (in force 1 July 2024): s.132 = advocate-client privilege (equivalent of IEA ss.126-128 as consolidated); s.133 = privilege not waived by volunteering evidence; s.134 = confidential communications with legal advisers. Reported content of s.132: advocate may not disclose client communications or advice unless client expressly consents; exceptions for communications in furtherance of an illegal purpose and facts observed showing a crime or fraud since the engagement began; survives end of engagement. [s.132 content: VERIFIED-SECONDARY via https://vidhijudicial.com/section-132-of-the-bharatiya-sakshya-adhiniyam,-2023.html ; s.133/134 operative text NOT read. Mapping to old IEA section numbers is [INFERENCE]; REVIEW]
- **In Re: Summoning Advocates who give legal opinion or represent parties during investigation of cases (Suo Motu Writ Petition (Crl.) No. 2 of 2025), 2025 INSC 1275**, SC, 31 Oct 2025 (CJI Gavai, Vinod Chandran, N.V. Anjaria JJ.). Held [as reported]: investigators cannot summon an advocate merely for representing/advising an accused; summons must state the s.132 exception relied on and have written approval of an officer not below SP; privilege belongs to the client, invocable by advocate; document production disputes go before a court; in-house counsel are not "advocates" for s.132 but may get s.134 protection. Secondary reports vary on how far s.134 protects in-house counsel. [VERIFIED-SECONDARY: https://www.verdictum.in/court-updates/supreme-court/in-re-summoning-advocates-who-give-legal-opinion-2025-insc-1275-in-house-counsel-privilege-1596424 ; https://www.scobserver.in/journal/summoning-advocates-who-represent-clients-under-investigation-judgement-summary/ ; https://law.drishtiias.com/current-affairs/advocates-cannot-be-summoned-for-merely-representing-clients-except-under-section-132-bsa ; judgment text not read; REVIEW]
- Commentary argues no privilege can arise between a user and an AI chatbot as it is not an enrolled advocate; this is commentary, not a holding. [VERIFIED-SECONDARY: https://www.barandbench.com/view-point/legal-privilege-in-the-era-of-generative-ai]

### Product implications (INFERENCE; REVIEW)
- Treat all client matter data as privileged by default; privilege is the client's, so any vendor/sub-processor access, support-staff access, or model-training use of matter content is a potential waiver/confidentiality-breach argument. Engineering controls: tenant isolation, encryption, no training on customer content, audit logs, strict staff access, legal-hold/export tooling.
- Build "privilege-resistant" export/production workflows: when an agency demands documents, the advocate should be able to produce under court supervision; avoid product features that auto-share with third parties.
- The platform should not itself hold itself out as a "legal adviser" to users (privilege will not attach to platform communications; see UPL below).
- Document that in-house-counsel users may have a different privilege position.

## 3. Personal data and cyber rules

### 3.1 DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025

- DPDP Rules 2025 were published in the Gazette in mid-November 2025. Sources give 13 or 14 November 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E) cited; a corrigendum G.S.R. 892(E) of 10 Dec 2025 was reported). Date to be confirmed on egazette. [VERIFIED-SECONDARY: https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/12/26/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-key-highlights/ ; https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/breaking-govt-notifies-final-dpdp-rules-sets-staggered-rollout-with-key-obligations-for-data-fiduciaries-84201.htm ; https://dpdp.myndsolution.com/wiki/guides/key-dates-and-commencement/ (search snippet)]

[VERIFIED-SECONDARY: dpdpa.com mirror https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdparules/rule1.html quoting the Gazette; official PDF not read]
- Act sections that commence with the 18-month tranche (sources say) include s.44(2), which omits IT Act s.43A. [VERIFIED-SECONDARY: https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/what-happens-spdi-rules-after-dpdp-enforcement/ ; https://www.sansalegal.com/post/how-to-prepare-for-the-omission-of-section-43a-of-the-information-technology-act ; REVIEW: confirm which Act sections were brought into force on which date via the commencement notification]
- **Rule 7 breach intimation** (text summarised via mirror): intimate each affected Data Principal without delay (through account/registered contact, plain language, nature/extent/timing, consequences, mitigation, contact); intimate the Board without delay, then detailed report within **72 hours** (extendable by the Board on written request). [VERIFIED-SECONDARY: https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdparules/rule7.html]
- **Rule 15 cross-border (text read via mirror)**: personal data processed under the Act "may be transferred outside the territory of India" subject to requirements the Central Government may specify, by general or special order, for making it available to any foreign State or entity under its control. This is the "permit unless restricted" model, not a whitelist. [VERIFIED-SECONDARY: https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdparules/rule15.html ; Act s.16 permits transfers except to countries notified as restricted: INFERENCE, not re-read]
- **Rule 13 (SDF)**: Significant Data Fiduciaries may have to keep specified personal data and traffic data in India per a committee's recommendations; additional SDF duties (audit, DPIA) per Act s.10. [VERIFIED-SECONDARY (search summary); the text of Rule 13 was inconsistently reproduced across sources; REVIEW]
- **Children (Rule 10, as reported)**: verifiable parental consent, with methods including DigiLocker-based checks; bars tracking/behavioural monitoring/targeted ads directed at children. Minimal relevance for a lawyer-facing product except in client records of minors (e.g. family/POCSO matters). [VERIFIED-SECONDARY: https://www.seclore.com/fundamentals/dpdp-rules-2025-compliance-guide/]
- **Log retention**: sources agree on a one-year minimum retention of logs/relevant personal data for breach detection but disagree on the rule number (Rule 6 vs 8). [VERIFIED-SECONDARY; REVIEW]
- Penalties: the Act's Schedule provides penalties up to the order of Rs 250 crore for security-safeguard failure; not re-read this session. [INFERENCE; REVIEW]
- Roles: an advocate/law firm is likely a Data Fiduciary for client data; the platform vendor is likely a Data Processor (contract under s.8(2) required) but may be a joint/independent fiduciary for its own account, analytics and CRM-marketing data. Consent Manager provisions are optional intermediary infrastructure. [INFERENCE; REVIEW]
- Exemptions in Act s.17 (e.g. for enforcing legal rights/claims, judicial functions) may apply to some litigation-related processing; scope is untested. [INFERENCE; REVIEW]

### 3.2 IT Act s.43A and SPDI Rules 2011

- s.43A and the SPDI Rules remain the operative regime until the DPDP s.44(2) omission takes effect (reported 13 May 2027); the SPDI Rules are made under s.43A and would lose footing; whether any savings clause applies is unclear. [VERIFIED-SECONDARY: ksandk and sansalegal links above; REVIEW]
- Until then, design to satisfy both: SPDI covers "sensitive personal data" (passwords, financial info, health, etc.) and requires a privacy policy, consent, and reasonable security practices (ISO 27001 is the named example, recalled). [INFERENCE]

### 3.3 CERT-In Directions of 28 April 2022 (issued under IT Act s.70B(6))

Read from the official PDF (https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf, saved and parsed locally) [VERIFIED-PRIMARY]:
- (i) Sync ICT system clocks to NIC/NPL NTP servers.
- (ii) Report listed cyber incidents (Annexure I) to CERT-In **within 6 hours** of noticing them.
- (iv) Enable logs of all ICT systems and keep them securely for a **rolling 180 days**, maintained **within Indian jurisdiction**.
- Further direction: data centres, VPS/cloud/VPN providers must keep subscriber/registration records for **5 years or longer**.
- Applies to "service providers, intermediaries, data centres, body corporate and Government organisations".
- Non-compliance consequence: s.70B(7) (up to one year imprisonment/fine, per FAQ commentary). [VERIFIED-SECONDARY: https://www.ahlawatassociates.com/news/cert-in-releases-faqs-on-directions-on-cyber-security]
- Effective date: reports differ (60 days after issue, i.e. about 27 June 2022). [VERIFIED-SECONDARY]

### Product implications (INFERENCE; REVIEW)
- Platform is a "body corporate"/intermediary: needs a 6-hour CERT-In incident reporting runbook AND a DPDP breach runbook (Board + each affected principal; 72-hour detailed report) from mid-May 2027. Log retention should satisfy the longest of 180 days (India-hosted) and DPDP's one year.
- Host primary data and logs in India (also helps privilege/confidentiality narrative and future SDF localisation).
- Draft DPA/processor terms so the advocate remains the controller of client data. Provide breach-notification pass-through to advocates.
- Collect only needed data; avoid ingesting sensitive client files into marketing/CRM features; separate consent for any marketing use (and see section 1: marketing to prospects is the problem anyway).
- Rule 4 Consent Manager regime goes live in about a month; irrelevant unless the product plans to be/integrate a consent manager.

### Product implications (INFERENCE; REVIEW)
- Position the OS as a tool for enrolled advocates (professional user in the loop), not as a consumer-facing legal-advice engine.
- Hard requirement: every AI-generated citation must be machine-verified against a trusted source (SCC/Manupatra-like licensed DB, official judgment portals, or user-supplied PDFs) and flagged "unverified" otherwise; show provenance; make "verify before filing" a visible step; log what was generated and checked (useful against misconduct allegations).
- No auto-filing or auto-submission of AI-drafted pleadings without an explicit human sign-off.
- Do not send client-confidential matter to third-party model APIs without contractual no-training/no-retention terms and India-resident or approved processing; disclose model sub-processors.
- Add disclosures where courts/state bars start requiring AI-use declarations (Kerala-style audit trails are a design hint).
- Monitor the BCI AI committee (directed July 2026) because new binding norms could land at any time.

## 5. Payments, billing, GST

- **GST on legal services**: Notification 13/2017-Central Tax (Rate), Sl. No. 2 (reverse charge): services by an individual advocate (including a senior advocate) by way of representational services before any court, tribunal or authority, and legal services by a firm of advocates, supplied to a **business entity** located in the taxable territory are under **reverse charge**, i.e. the recipient business pays GST. CBIC clarified in July 2017 that "legal services" means advice, consultancy or assistance in any branch of law including representational services; the J.K. Mittal (Delhi HC) comma controversy was clarified by the Government. ## 6. Court data reuse

- **Eastern Book Company v. D.B. Modak, (2008) 1 SCC 1** (SC, decided Dec 2007 per most sources): rejected "sweat of the brow" as sufficient; adopted "skill and judgment" with a "minimal degree of creativity"; protected SCC's headnotes, editorial notes, footnotes and editorially created elements; the text of judgments themselves is not copyrightable by the publisher; reproduction of judgments falls within Copyright Act s.52(1)(q)(iv) (reported). [UNVERIFIED; REVIEW: read ecourts.gov.in, services.ecourts.gov.in, judgments.ecourts.gov.in, sci.gov.in terms and robots.txt; also the Delhi HC/other HC portals]

### Product implications (INFERENCE; REVIEW)
- Safe core: store/serve raw judgment text from official sources; link out to official PDFs.
- Avoid copying commercial headnotes, SCC/Manupatra/AIR summaries, editorial paragraph labels, or commercial citation databases. If using licensed content, check the licence for AI/embedding use.

- Personal data inside judgments (victims, minors, matrimonial/POCSO parties) is still personal data under DPDP once in force; avoid building people-search or profiling over case data. [INFERENCE; REVIEW]
