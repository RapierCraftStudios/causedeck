## 1. Market size

### 1.1 Enrolled advocates
- The Supreme Court's 10 Apr 2023 judgment in the verification case records BCI's counter-affidavit: about 20.57 lakh enrolled advocates in the tabulated statement, with about 7.55 lakh verification forms received (9.22 lakh total received per another line). The Court also records that the count "stood at 16 lakh" when verification began and is "estimated to be almost 25.70 lakh" at the time of judgment. [VERIFIED] https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2023/2478/2478_2023_1_28_43311_Judgement_10-Apr-2023.pdf (accessed 2026-10-17)
- Reading: the "official" national headcount is between roughly 20.6 and 25.7 lakh (2.06-2.57 million), and BCI itself suspects a share are not qualified or hold fake degrees. A majority of enrolled advocates had not submitted verification forms as of that judgment. [VERIFIED, same URL]
- Rajya Sabha answer (19 Dec 2024): UP Bar Council alone had 4,69,900 advocates registered (till 23 Nov 2024), 49,454 female. [VERIFIED] https://legalaffairs.gov.in/sites/default/files/AU2827.pdf (accessed 2026-10-17)
- A PTI report of Dec 2025 cites 5,14,439 advocates "enrolled" with a council of whom 2,49,809 were issued certificates of practice. The search summary suspected this is the UP Bar Council figure, not national; unconfirmed. [REPORTED, UNCERTAIN] Query surfaced via https://www.siasat.com/advocates-with-criminal-records-face-licence-suspension-up-bar-council-to-hc-3314406/amp/ (accessed 2026-10-17)
- [INFERENCE] If the certificate-of-practice ratio (about 49%) from that UP-style figure generalises, "actively practising" advocates may be roughly half of enrolled, i.e. a serviceable base of about 10-13 lakh. This is a weak extrapolation from one unverified datum; do not rely on it.
- Verification-of-practice drive: BCI's 2023 verification requirement and the Kerala HC holding (Jan 2025) that bar councils cannot collect the Rs 2,500 verification fee directed by BCI in 2017. [REPORTED] https://www.livelaw.in/amp/high-court/kerala-high-court/kerala-high-court-enrollment-fees-verification-of-certificate-279783 (accessed 2026-10-17)

### 1.2 Women share and state distribution
- Lok Sabha unstarred Q1034 (25 Jul 2025): about 2,84,507 women advocates enrolled "across the country" based on consolidated data from fifteen State Bar Councils (so not complete); UP 49,545 women as of Nov 2024; BCI does not maintain caste-wise data. [VERIFIED] https://legalaffairs.gov.in/sites/default/files/AU1034.pdf (accessed 2026-10-17)
- Another article puts women at about 15% of enrolled advocates (2023-era figure). [REPORTED] https://theleaflet.in/international-womens-day/towards-an-inclusive-bar-why-indias-women-lawyers-are-litigating-their-way-into-the-bar (accessed 2026-10-17). Note this is inconsistent with the 2.84 lakh figure if the national total is ~20-25 lakh (would be 11-14%); both are in the same range. [INFERENCE] Women are roughly 11-15% of the enrolled Bar, while law-school enrolment is near parity (per an opinion piece, same Leaflet link) so women are likely over-represented among *new* entrants and attrit afterwards.
- SC (2025) ordered 30% of State Bar Council seats for women advocates. [REPORTED] https://www.dailypioneer.com/2025/india/sc-orders-30-seats-for-women-in-bar-councils.html (accessed 2026-10-17)
- State distribution: only UP is confirmed (about 4.7-5.1 lakh, i.e. roughly a fifth to a quarter of the national total [INFERENCE]). I did NOT find a state-wise table. Gap.

### 1.3 AIBE (entry gate to practice) numbers
- AIBE XIX (Dec 2024 exam, results Mar 2025): 2,36,403 registered, 2,29,843 appeared, 1,77,592 passed (77.27%), 52,251 failed; AIBE XVIII fail rate was 51.64% per the same RTI-based reports. [REPORTED, RTI-sourced] https://www.barandbench.com/news/aibe-xix-pass-rate-increased-from-51-to-77-rti-reply-reveals and https://lexibal.com/aibe-xix-pass-rate-soars-to-77/ (accessed 2026-10-17)
- AIBE XX: about 2,51,968 appeared, pass rate about 69.21%, result dated 7 Jan 2026. [REPORTED, aggregator] https://collegedunia.com/exams/aibe/results (accessed 2026-10-17)
- [INFERENCE] About 1.2-1.8 lakh new certificate-eligible advocates per year (if ~70% of ~2.3-2.5 lakh appearing pass). This is the annual inflow of the target segment (age ~22-28). Note multiple AIBE attempts and repeat takers mean this overstates unique new graduates.

### 1.4 Law schools and graduates
- BCI Rules of Legal Education (Three-Year Moratorium) 2025 freeze new law colleges and extra sections for three years from 14 Aug 2025; a BCI release as summarised by an education site says about 2,000 law colleges operate. [REPORTED, secondary] https://educationforallinindia.com/bcis-2025-moratorium-on-new-law-colleges/ and https://www.legalkart.com/en/legal-blog/bci-freezes-approval-of-new-law-colleges-for-3-years-what-it-means-for-legal-education-in-india (accessed 2026-10-17)
- Law graduates per year: NOT found as a sourced figure. [INFERENCE] Order of magnitude should track AIBE registrants (~2.3-2.5 lakh/yr), which includes backlog candidates.
- Share of young lawyers: NOT found as a sourced figure. Vidhi's HC advocate survey (2,800 advocates, 8 High Courts) is the best distribution source; see 2.1.

### 1.5 Courts, judges, pendency
- 25 High Courts. HC sanctioned strength 1,114 rising to 1,122 (Dec 2024-Feb 2025 parliamentary figures); subordinate judiciary sanctioned strength 25,741 (as of 9 Dec 2024) with about 5,262 vacancies. [VERIFIED] https://rsdebate.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/753144/1/PQ_266_19122024_U2820_p308_p311.pdf and https://ianslive.in/367-vacancies-of-judges-in-high-courts-arjun-ram-meghwal--20250213154204 (accessed 2026-10-17)
- Pendency (NJDG via Parliament, mid-2026): subordinate courts about 4.99 crore (Lok Sabha 4,99,16,653; up from 4.77 crore in 2025), HCs about 64-65 lakh (64,67,391 Lok Sabha vs 63.76 lakh another report), Supreme Court about 92,755 to 95,936 as of 28 Jul 2026. Two sources disagree slightly; treat as approximate. [REPORTED] https://ianslive.in/over-476-crore-cases-pending-across-indian-courts-govt-tells-parliament--20260213184542 and https://www.barandbench.com/news/26-cases-pending-in-supreme-court-for-30-years-558-cases-for-20-years-centre-in-rajya-sabha (accessed 2026-10-17)

- Tribunals: number and caseload NOT researched. Gap.

## 2. Young-lawyer economics

### 2.1 Incomes
- Vidhi JALDI survey (2,800 advocates, High Courts): cited in a Delhi HC PIL as finding "more than 79% of the advocates across seven High Courts with less than two years of legal practice at the Bar earn less than Rs 10,000 per month". [REPORTED via court coverage; I did not locate the figure in the report text, and the survey covered eight HCs] https://www.barandbench.com/news/delhi-high-court-appeals-senior-lawyers-pay-good-stipend-juniors and https://vidhilegalpolicy.in/research/a-survey-of-advocates-practicing-before-the-high-courts/ (accessed 2026-10-17)
- Delhi pilot of that survey: 80% of Delhi HC respondents believed first-two-year lawyers earn Rs 5,000-20,000/month; a Rs 2,000-5,000 band added after pilot drew >40% of responses. [REPORTED] https://barandbench.com/columns/financial-well-being-of-advocates-is-not-all-black-and-white (accessed 2026-10-17)
- Chamber pay anecdotes (J&K): junior associates Rs 20-25k, senior associates Rs 40-50k per month at one senior's chamber; another pays juniors Rs 15-25k. Single-region column, not a survey. [REPORTED] https://www.barandbench.com/amp/story/columns/litigation-columns/what-do-senior-lawyers-pay-their-junior-advocates-in-jammu-kashmir (accessed 2026-10-17)

### 2.2 Stipend rules and directions
- BCI circular (15 Oct 2024): suggested minimum Rs 20,000/month (urban) and Rs 15,000/month (rural) for juniors for at least three years; framed as guidance, not mandatory. [REPORTED] https://www.barandbench.com/news/bci-suggests-20k-stipend-junior-advocates-urban-15k-juniors-in-rural-areas and https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2024/10/16/bci-issues-guidelines-to-pay-minimum-stipend-to-junior-advocates/ (accessed 2026-10-17)
- Delhi HC dismissed a PIL (Simran Kumari) seeking a mandamus for stipends, saying it could only appeal to seniors. [REPORTED] https://lawbeat.in/news-updates/seniors-law-profession-ensure-stipend-paid-juniors-enough-delhi-high-court (accessed 2026-10-17)
- Madras HC fixed Rs 20,000 (Chennai/Madurai/Coimbatore) and Rs 15,000 elsewhere in TN/Puducherry; Arvind Datar criticised it for lacking data. [REPORTED] https://deccanchronicle.com/nation/pay-young-lawyers-a-monthly-stipend-of-15-20k-madras-hc-898576 and https://www.barandbench.com/columns/legal-notes-by-arvind-datar-mandatory-fees-for-junior-advocates (accessed 2026-10-17)
- MP HC (28 Sep 2026, per search result) directed State Bar Council to frame stipend guidelines within six months, noting the BCI circular is only recommendatory. [REPORTED] https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/madhya-pradesh-high-court/madhya-pradesh-high-court-directs-state-bar-council-to-frame-guidelines-for-minimum-stipend-for-junior-advocates-552254 (accessed 2026-10-17)
- Bombay HC (Kolhapur bench) issued notice on a petition by 21 young advocates; one report cites a Supreme Court judgment "Sarika Tyagi v. Union of India" (19 Jun 2026) which I could NOT verify. Do not cite it. [REPORTED, UNVERIFIED] https://supremetoday.ai/bombay-high-court-issues-notice-on-stipend-for-junior-advocates-citing-supreme-court-observations-20260905063 (accessed 2026-10-17)
- Kerala pays eligible young lawyers Rs 3,000/month (noted by a P&H HC bench). [REPORTED] https://desikaanoon.in/punjab-haryana-high-court-seeks-bci-state-bar-council-response-on-plea-for-minimum-stipend-for-junior-lawyers/ (accessed 2026-10-17)
- CJI Chandrachud (Nov 2022): "junior lawyers are not slaves", pay decent salaries. [REPORTED] https://www.livelaw.in/amp/top-stories/junior-lawyers-are-not-slaves-pay-them-decent-salaries-legal-profession-should-not-be-an-old-boys-club-cji-dy-chandrachud-214566 (accessed 2026-10-17)
- Academic interview study (55 juniors, Delhi HC and district courts) describes little/no pay for years. [REPORTED] https://www.ijllr.com/post/from-law-school-to-litigation-barriers-mentorship-gaps-and-the-sustainability-crisis-facing-india (accessed 2026-10-17)

### 2.3 Willingness to pay, payments, devices
- Manupatra Academy survey (May 9-19, 2025, n=227; ~37% students, ~24% advocates, ~12% in-house; online/social distribution so not representative): top adoption enablers were training/certification (~67%) and free trial or freemium access (~67%); legal research was the most-used AI tool category (~78%); named barriers were cost, ethics, shortage of skills. [REPORTED] https://manupatracademy.com/Home/survey-reports and https://www.barandbench.com/news/ai-adoption-and-impact-legal-industry-indian-perspective (accessed 2026-10-17)
- [INFERENCE] With median juniors earning under Rs 10k/month, a viable price point is likely under about Rs 100-300/month or freemium with pay-per-use; anything above a few hundred rupees competes with phone recharge/data. Unvalidated; needs a pricing test.
- UPI usage and smartphone/laptop ownership among young lawyers: NOT found. Gap. [INFERENCE] UPI is near-universal among urban 21-30 year olds in India generally, but this is general knowledge, not sourced here.

## 3. Workflows

### 3.1 Case tracking
- Official channels: eCourts Services (web + mobile app) search by CNR (16-character ID), case number, party, advocate name, FIR; CAPTCHA required on web. [REPORTED] https://services.india.gov.in/service/detail/check-case-status-of-supreme-court-and-high-courts-1 and https://en.vikaspedia.in/viewcontent/e-governance/online-legal-services/how-to-check-court-case-status-online-in-india-a-complete-guide-to-ecourts-cnr-number-case-number-and-case-history?lgn=en (accessed 2026-10-17)
- eCourts app features: "My Cases" with automatic updates, advocate-wise cause list generation, search by advocate name. [REPORTED] https://www.apkmirror.com/apk/nic-egov-mobile-apps/ecourts-services/ and https://orissahighcourt.nic.in/ecourts-mobile-app/eCourts_Services_Mobile_App_English.pdf (accessed 2026-10-17)
- Private tracking tools (vendor claims, unverified): Court Click, LexTrack, Mike Litigator (claims 10,000+ courts), India Case Status (WhatsApp/email/app alerts, calendar export), iCourts Law, CaseWatch. [REPORTED, MARKETING] https://www.legaltechnologyhub.com/vendors/india-case-status-by-questlore-indian-gamer-media-llp/ and https://chrome-stats.com/d/com.icourtslaw and https://directory.lawnext.com/products/casewatch/ (accessed 2026-10-17). Their existence shows a crowded low-end tracker market. [INFERENCE]
- Clerk/munshi WhatsApp groups, display boards, physical cause-list checking: I found NO sourced evidence in this session. [INFERENCE from general domain knowledge, not sourced] these remain primary in district courts where eCourts data lags. Needs interviews.

### 3.2 Adjournments and hearing management
- DAKSH time-and-motion study (4 Bangalore courts): adjournments consumed about 54% of civil and 33% of criminal sitting time. Small sample, 2016. [REPORTED] https://dakshindia.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/DAKSH-TIME-AND-MOTION-STUDY-OF-FOUR-DISTRICT-AND-SESSIONS-COURTS.pdf (accessed 2026-10-17)
- CJI Chandrachud (Nov 2023): 154 adjournments per miscellaneous day cited; plea against "tareekh pe tareekh". [REPORTED] https://www.livelaw.in/amp/top-stories/dont-want-this-to-be-a-tareekh-pe-tareekh-court-cji-dy-chandrachud-urges-lawyers-to-not-seek-unnecessary-adjournments-241510 (accessed 2026-10-17)
- [INFERENCE] Because the next date changes constantly and is the single most decision-critical datum, "next-date accuracy plus reminders" is the core job-to-be-done.

### 3.3 E-filing, virtual hearings, certified copies
- eCourts e-Filing 3.0 launched Apr 2021: advocate registration, adding partners/clients, online vakalatnama, pleading templates, digital signing; rolled out state by state at district level; portal filing.ecourts.gov.in. [REPORTED] https://www.highcourtchd.gov.in/sub_pages/left_menu/newsletter/pdf_files/Newsletter-%20April%202021.pdf and https://dhalaitrp.dcourts.gov.in/?p=6574 (accessed 2026-10-17)
- SC: CJI Gavai (Nov 2025) announced a new e-filing version piloted covering e-filing, certified copies and hybrid hearings under one login; old and new run in parallel. LiveLaw article paywalled; status unconfirmed. [REPORTED] https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/cji-br-gavai-announces-launch-of-new-version-of-e-filing-portal-310612 (accessed 2026-10-17)
- Drafting, Limitation Act deadline calculation, document version management, client intake, billing/fee collection, confidentiality practice: NO sourced material found on actual practitioner workflow. These sections are gaps; recommendations below are [INFERENCE] only.
- Confidentiality: nothing lawyer-specific found for DPDP Rules 2025 (notified 14 Nov 2025, phased: some rules immediate, then 12 and 18 months). [REPORTED] https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf and https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/in/pdf/2025/12/dpdp-act-2023-and-dpdp-rules-2025-simplified.pdf (accessed 2026-10-17). [INFERENCE] Client-privilege (BSA s.132-134 equivalents of old Evidence Act s.126-129; section numbers from my background knowledge, verify) and data-localisation/vendor questions will matter for any cloud product; check before design.

### 3.4 Research workflow
- Manupatra survey: research tools were the most-used AI category (~78%) - see 2.3. No data found on Indian Kanoon vs SCC Online vs Manupatra usage shares. Gap.
- AI-fabricated citations are now a disciplinary risk: SC in Gummadi Usha Rani v. Sure Mallikarjuna Rao (2025) flagged a trial court relying on four non-existent judgments; Pooja Ramesh Singh v. J&K Bank (2026 INSC 668) held citing unverified AI-generated judgments is misconduct and asked BCI to form a committee on AI in litigation. [REPORTED] https://www.medianama.com/2026/03/223-supreme-court-ai-fake-judgments-menace/ and https://blog.ipleaders.in/ai-hallucinated-case-law-fake-citations-india/ (accessed 2026-10-17)
- [INFERENCE] Product implication: any AI feature must show verifiable source links to the real judgment (eSCR/HC site/Indian Kanoon) and force a "verified" step.

## 4. eCourts Phase III and official tech

- Phase III: Cabinet approved 13 Sep 2023, Rs 7,210 crore over four years; components include digitisation of records Rs 2,038.4 cr, cloud Rs 1,205.23 cr, hardware Rs 643.66 cr, new-court infrastructure Rs 426.25 cr, 1,150 virtual courts Rs 413.08 cr, 4,400 eSewa Kendras Rs 394.48 cr; aims at paperless courts and data-driven scheduling. [VERIFIED/official release] https://www.pmindia.gov.in/?p=16276489 and https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2023/sep/doc2023922255101.pdf (accessed 2026-10-17)
- SC tools: SUVAS (AI translation) and SUPACE (research assistant). In 2023 SC translated over 31,000 of ~36,000 judgments on the e-SCR portal; sources differ on language coverage (9 vs 18). Patna HC launched a SUVAS Cell portal (4 Jul 2025); Allahabad HC reported 1 lakh Hindi translations (5 Oct 2026). Law Minister told Rajya Sabha (Mar 2025) SC is integrating AI/ML into case management, transcription, translation. [REPORTED] https://indialegallive.com/cover-story-articles/il-feature-news/artificial-intelligence-translation-vernacular-languages/ and https://www.livelaw.in/news-updates/allahabad-high-court-makes-1-lakh-judgments-available-hindi-through-ai-assisted-translation-554106 and https://www.livelaw.in/amp/news-updates/patna-high-court-launches-dedicated-web-portal-suvas-cell-297073 (accessed 2026-10-17)
- Official public API for case data: I found NO official documented public API. The official surface is portal/app with CAPTCHA; commercial resellers (Surepass, Meon) sell "eCourts APIs" with undisclosed sourcing. [REPORTED] https://surepass.io/ecourts-api/ and https://meon.co.in/ecourts-api (accessed 2026-10-17). [INFERENCE] A product built on scraping eCourts would carry terms-of-use, CAPTCHA and reliability risk; terms not reviewed. Do a legal review and check for a sanctioned data-sharing route (e.g. eCommittee/NIC) before committing.
- Data quality: records are court-updated, completeness varies by court (vikaspedia link above). No quantified study found. Gap.

## 6. Distribution channels

- Advertising constraint (critical): Rule 36 (Ch. II, Part VI, BCI Rules 2009) bars advocates from soliciting/advertising. BCI's March 2025 statement condemned social-media promotion, influencer and celebrity endorsement of legal services and legal-influencer misinformation, and directed State Bar Councils to start disciplinary proceedings; Bar Council of Delhi issued a similar notice Aug 2025 (misconduct under s.35 Advocates Act). Madras HC (P.N. Vignesh v. BCI, 2024) denied online legal-ad platforms safe harbour. [REPORTED] https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/03/17/bci-condemns-warns-against-unethical-legal-advertising-advocates-legal-influencers-issues-directives/ and https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/08/07/lgd11-bar-body-notice.html and https://www.medianama.com/2025/03/223-the-bar-council-of-india-warns-against-legal-advertising-online/ (accessed 2026-10-17)
- [INFERENCE] Implication: market the *tool* to lawyers (B2B productivity), never lawyers' services to the public via the tool; avoid features that let users advertise or generate leads. Do not engage lawyers as paid influencers.
- Channel candidates (all [INFERENCE], no sourced data on reach this session): law-school moot/clinic/placement communities; CLAT/AIBE prep communities (AIBE has ~2.3-2.5 lakh annual candidates, a natural funnel); State/district bar association WhatsApp/Telegram groups; legal-education creators (ethically teaching, not soliciting); campus ambassadors. Manupatra's survey shows the distribution path institutions use (social media, email, institutional channels).

## Confidence & unknowns
High-ish confidence: AIBE order of magnitude (2.3-2.5 lakh candidates/year, 70-77% pass); national enrolment is roughly 2-2.6 million with an unverified-practice cloud; pendency about 5+ crore subordinate and ~65 lakh HC; eCourts Phase III Rs 7,210 cr; BCI stipend guideline Rs 20k/15k is advisory; advertising rules restrict go-to-market.
Medium: junior income (<Rs 10k for most under-2-year lawyers) relies on a petition's characterisation of Vidhi, not the report; women share of 11-15%; 2,000 law colleges.
Low/unknown (need primary research): actual share of Gen Z (21-30) among advocates; state-wise distribution; law graduates per year; tribunal counts; real practitioner workflows (clerk/munshi WhatsApp, cause list habits, Limitation calculation, drafting, version control, billing, client intake, confidentiality); Indian Kanoon vs SCC/Manupatra usage; smartphone/laptop/UPI among young lawyers; willingness to pay; official API availability and eCourts terms of use; data-quality metrics; Reddit/LinkedIn voices; in-house and law-firm associate workflows.
Source quality warnings: my tool returned summaries, not page text; two items (Sarika Tyagi SC judgment; BSA section numbers) are unverified; some parliamentary figures differ slightly across sources. Recommended next step: 15-20 interviews with junior litigators in 3-4 cities and a fetch of the primary Parliament/NJDG/BCI documents.
