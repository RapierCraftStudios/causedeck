## 2. Workflow gaps: what the evidence says

Honest summary first: no practitioner-run survey of how Indian juniors track dates, use clerks, handle fees or confidentiality exists in what I could reach. What follows is a mix of one academic account, newspaper columns, personal essays, and vendor claims. Treat vendor claims as hypotheses.

### (a) Cause lists, item numbers, next dates
- Cause lists are published the evening before; a typical entry has serial/item number, case number, parties, advocates, court hall and judge, with stage headings (admission, part-heard) and supplementary lists that can add a missing case. [REPORTED, summary of a tribunal rule and cause-list explainers] https://www.aptel.gov.in/old_website/pdf/chapter%20IV.pdf and https://advocategandhi.com/who-prepares-the-cause-list-understanding-the-process-importance-and-legal-framework-behind-court-cause-lists-in-india/ (accessed 2026-10-17)
- Bar & Bench column recalls a family chamber where the arrival of the cause list was awaited by clerk and juniors "greater than that for any meal or parcel"; then "files were pulled out, notes arranged, law books opened". It is a memoir of older practice, not a current survey. https://www.barandbench.com/columns/the-quiet-power-of-the-cause-list (accessed 2026-10-17)
- Clerks check cause lists, cure registry objections, collect certified copies and are "the bridge between advocates and registries" (Bar & Bench column on advocates' clerks; it gives no numbers, nothing on diaries or fees). [REPORTED] https://www.barandbench.com/columns/recognising-the-invisible-institution-of-indian-justice (accessed 2026-10-17)
- Late or unreliable publication is a long-running complaint. Search results report that in 2014 the SC Bar Association complained cause-list uploads came near 10 pm and the option was non-operational 95 percent of the time, with the Registrar's reply being to use smartphones; in 2024 the SC AoR Association asked that next-day hearing sequence be published in advance. [REPORTED, search-summary only; I did not open them] https://www.legallyindia.com/the-bench-and-the-bar/sc-asks-scba-to-access-website-on-smart-phones-20140711-4872 and https://www.greaterkashmir.com/article/lawyers-body-writes-to-cji-about-hearing-sequence-in-sc/353389 (the m. link redirected and I did not re-fetch) (accessed 2026-10-17). Also DAKSH reports Karnataka HC advocates get about a day's notice before final hearings: https://dakshindia.org/advance-causelist-petition-high-court-of-karnataka (accessed 2026-10-17)
- Item number tracking: no source describes how juniors follow live item progression (for example, "item 37, court is at item 22"). [INFERENCE] This is a genuine, frequent anxiety in high courts and big district complexes where lists run to 60-100+ items and a missed call can mean a dismissal or adverse order; it points to a live "court position / my item distance" alert as a high-value feature, but needs interviews. Evidence of 60+ matters per court: a Legally India thread estimate of 18 courtrooms with at least 60 matters each, anonymous and unverified. https://www.legallyindia.com/convos/topic/325702-h (accessed 2026-10-17)

### (b) Munshis, clerks, WhatsApp groups
- Academic baseline: Journal of Asian Studies, vol 31 no 2 (Feb 1972): munshis, employed by vakils mainly at district and subdivisional courts, look after case documents, complete court applications, copy cause lists from magistrates' clerks, update the vakil's diary of pending cases, and run errands (often personal); the role is undefined, lawyers can practise without one, munshis hop between employers and the occupation is economically precarious. Fifty-plus years old; current relevance [INFERENCE: plausible, unconfirmed]. https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-asian-studies/article/munshis-and-their-masters-the-organization-of-an-occupational-relationship-in-the-indian-legal-system/50A5768E4307D38BDB08CB86EB2F11E8 (accessed 2026-10-17)
- Legal framework: West Bengal Law Clerks Act 1997 and a Uttarakhand HC direction to frame clerk-welfare legislation indicate clerks are a recognised but loosely regulated class. [REPORTED via search summary of the B&B column and https://barandbench.com/uttarakhand-hc-directs-state-govt-frame-legislation-benefits-advocates-clerks/]
- Vendor claim on cost: Vakeel Saathi's Product Hunt page says a munshi costs each lawyer Rs 15,000-30,000 per month and often leaves within 18 months, and sells WhatsApp-driven "next date" queries, cause-list monitor and invoice nudges at Rs 799 / 1,999 / 4,999 per month. Maker's own claims; page has 6 followers, no written reviews. [REPORTED, MARKETING] https://www.producthunt.com/products/vakeel-saathi (accessed 2026-10-17). [INFERENCE] A Rs 15-30k munshi cost exceeds what most juniors earn (see 4.2), so most juniors share a senior's clerk or none; then the junior's "munshi" is the senior's, and the junior's own tracking is ad hoc.
- WhatsApp groups of advocates or clerks: no source found. Courts themselves are adding WhatsApp: SC integrated WhatsApp for case information on 25 Apr 2024 (CJI said it "will bring about a significant change in the working habits of lawyers and judges"); Kerala HC announced WhatsApp for communication with advocates and litigants. [REPORTED] https://www.barandbench.com/news/lawyers-and-litigants-supreme-court-case-information-on-whatsapp and https://www.barandbench.com/news/kerala-high-court-to-use-whatsapp-for-communication-with-advocates-litigants (accessed 2026-10-17). A Treelife note says some courts accept WhatsApp service of documents subject to proof of delivery. https://treelife.in/legal/validity-of-whatsapp-documents-as-court-service-a-changing-landscape (accessed 2026-10-17)

### (c) Physical diaries
- Vakalat Diary (small vendor) claims fewer than 5 percent of advocates use any digital tool for cases and that most rely on physical diaries, loose papers and memory. The vendor domain refused connection when I tried to fetch it; the claim comes from search snippets. [REPORTED, MARKETING, UNVERIFIED; the sample is not disclosed] https://vakalat.duckdns.org/about (accessed 2026-10-17). Same page cited in the summary: Pro plan Rs 400 per month; free tier up to five cases.
- Court-side diaries: tribunal rules require the court master to keep a "Court Diary" with next-sitting dates; this is the court's record, not the advocate's. [REPORTED] https://www.aptel.gov.in/old_website/pdf/chapter%20IV.pdf
- [INFERENCE] The diary is both calendar and client file index; an app must be as quick as writing a line, work offline in basement courtrooms, and support Hindi/regional names.

### (d) Limitation calculation
- No source describes how juniors compute limitation in practice. Legal doctrine that makes calculation error-prone: India House v. Kishan N. Lalwani (SC 2002) holds the certified-copy time is excluded under Section 12(2) even if the copy application is filed after limitation expires, no application is needed to claim s.12 benefit, and the judgment suggests adding the copy time to the prescribed period counting both application and delivery dates; A.D. Partha Sarathy v. State of AP reads the exclusion as operating only on events within the period. [REPORTED via search summary of the judgment text, not opened] https://www.legitquest.com/case/ms-india-house-v-kishan-n-lalwani/21A4 and https://www.legitquest.com/case/ad-partha-sarathy-v-state-of-andhra-pradesh/484a (accessed 2026-10-17)
- Existing tools: a limitation-period calculator is offered as a "free option" by Vakeel Saathi, so free calculators exist and are not a moat. [REPORTED] (Product Hunt link above)
- [INFERENCE] A calculator that outputs a defensible, cited computation (start date, exclusions, condonation under s.5, with statute and case citations) and stores the working per case is useful, but value is in the pipeline from "order date -> certified copy dates -> filing deadline reminders", not the arithmetic.

### (e) Drafting
- LawBhoomi: "Today, many young lawyers and law students rely heavily on old drafts, online templates, and copy-paste formats"; copy-paste from several drafts creates mismatched dates, unchanged names, annexures that do not support pleadings. Practitioners use precedents routinely; the issue is blind reuse. [REPORTED, blog] https://lawbhoomi.com/blindly-copying-legal-drafts-can-harm-your-case-in-these-5-ways/ (accessed 2026-10-17)
- Lawlex essay and a Legally India thread (an Indian drafting book request) show the same gap: templates bundles but no practical guidance. [REPORTED, search summary] https://www.legallyindia.com/convos/topic/473705-Good-books-on-drafting-in-the-Indian-context
- Tools: no source on Word vs Google Docs share. Vakeel Saathi says its drafting is grounded in BNS/BNSS text; one user says they still edit drafts themselves. [REPORTED]. [INFERENCE] MS Word on a laptop or a shared desktop in a chamber, files named by party and year in folders, is the likely default; consistency checks (name, date, annexure mismatches) are a concrete, low-AI feature supported by the LawBhoomi examples.
- New statutes (BNS/BNSS/BSA from July 2024) increase demand for section-mapping aids. [INFERENCE]

### (f) Fees, UPI, invoices, GST
- Income context only: Bar & Bench column citing survey data: 80 percent of Delhi respondents with up to two years' experience said juniors earn Rs 5,000-20,000 a month; in Calcutta and Gujarat 50 percent of respondents said under Rs 10,000; over 70 percent of Delhi respondents said senior advocates charge above Rs 1,00,000 per hearing. [REPORTED] https://www.barandbench.com/columns/financial-well-being-of-advocates-is-not-all-black-and-white (accessed 2026-10-17). It says nothing on how fees are collected.
- Another Bar & Bench column (via search summary) cites a 2015 B&B survey: 78 percent of first-generation and 85 percent of second-generation advocates with under five years earn under Rs 5 lakh a year; a 2020 Vidhi survey: 46 percent in five HCs said early-career lawyers earn under Rs 5,000. [REPORTED, not opened] https://www.barandbench.com/columns/experience-requirements-alone-cant-build-a-representative-bench
- Contingency fees are barred under BCI rules for litigation (client guide). [REPORTED] https://advocategandhi.com/decoding-lawyer-fees-in-india-what-every-client-must-know-before-hiring-legal-representation/
- GST: Finance Ministry's July 2017 clarification (via secondary pages) says legal services by individual advocates, including seniors, and firms of advocates to a business entity are under reverse charge (notification 13/2017-Central Tax (Rate)), so a business client pays the GST; the Bar & Bench article body failed to load, and I could not verify the threshold or current exemption position. [REPORTED, PARTIAL] https://itatonline.org/info/gst-on-legal-services-clarification-by-finance-ministry/ and https://taxscan.in/?p=9310 (search results; accessed 2026-10-17). [INFERENCE] Most district litigators serve individuals (B2C) and are small, so invoicing, GST and TDS are mainly an issue for those advising businesses or working as panel counsel; cash with handwritten receipts is probably common but no source found.
- UPI/cash habits of lawyers: nothing found. Vakeel Saathi markets "GST-aware invoicing, trust accounting and invoice nudges". [REPORTED, MARKETING]

## 6. Product-relevant synthesis [INFERENCE throughout]
1. Highest-frequency job: know tomorrow's list and my item position, record the next date after each hearing, and get a reminder; the evidence supports this indirectly (cause-list reverence, munshi diary duty, late publication complaints, pendency mostly criminal).
2. The munshi substitute is a real framing (vendor says Rs 15-30k a month), but juniors cannot afford it, so the pitch is "a free munshi-lite for the junior who shares or has no clerk".
3. Android-first, low-bandwidth, offline-tolerant, WhatsApp-adjacent (Android about 94 percent; UPI ubiquitous).
4. Limitation and certified-copy tracker, defect-cure tracker, and drafting consistency checks (names, dates, annexures) are concrete and not AI-hallucination-prone.
5. Price: free core, optional Rs 200-500 a month tier; research at the Indian Kanoon Rs 5,000/year anchor is the reference. Any AI research feature must show source links and a verify step (SC: misconduct to cite unverified AI judgments).
6. Do not advertise lawyers or generate leads (BCI 17 Mar 2025).
