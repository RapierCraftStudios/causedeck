## 3. Language

- Karnataka Kannada Language Comprehensive Development Act, 2022 (Karnataka Act 13 of 2023): PRS bill summary states "District Courts, Trial Courts, and Tribunals shall also conduct proceedings and pronounce judgements in Kannada"; English may be used for communication with SC/High Courts, Centre, etc. It repeals the Karnataka Official Language Act 1963 and Local Authorities (Official Language) Act 1981 [VERIFIED for the BILL text via PRS PDF read locally: https://PRSINDIA.org/files/bills_acts/bills_states/karnataka/2022/The Kannada Language Comprehensive Development Bill 2022 Bill Summary.pdf]. The enacted text (https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/19592/1/13of2023(e)_k_&_c.pdf) returned HTTP 403; search snippets indicate a section "Language in the Courts" (section 16 per a search summary) with the same effect [REPORTED]. Whether the section is in force/notified and how it is enforced was not established; a Karnataka HC single judge reportedly noted in the signboard litigation that the Act had not been gazetted as of that hearing (date unclear) [REPORTED, https://www.livelaw.in/amp/high-court/karnataka-high-court/karnataka-high-court-kannada-signboards-shops-sealing-stayed-273386]. Treat legal status as an open question.
- Constitutional frame: Art. 348 - HC proceedings/judgments in English unless Governor + President authorise another language [REPORTED via PRS notes].
- Lawyers' Literary Association petitioned the HC on 2 Nov 2022 to make Kannada compulsory in trial courts and allow arguments in Kannada; the report cites no statute [VERIFIED article read: https://barandbench.com/news/litigation/petition-before-karnataka-high-court-declare-kannada-compulsory-trial-courts]. This implies that, in practice, trial-court use of Kannada was not uniform as of late 2022 [INFERENCE].
- A Karnataka HC Division Bench pronounced an order in Kannada (Nanjavudootha Swamiji v. S Linganna, reported 12 Dec 2024) - noted as a departure from English [VERIFIED article read: https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/karnataka-high-court-bench-pronounces-order-in-kannada].
- HC AI-assisted translation project: Kannada judgments webpages (see 1.4); Nov 2024 notice invited EOIs for English-to-Kannada machine translation tool [REPORTED, search snippet of karnatakajudiciary notifications page]. No evidence found that SUVAS is deployed in Karnataka district courts.
- Prevalence figures (share of Kannada vs English orders/pleadings in Bengaluru courts): NOT found. Bengaluru's civil/commercial/NCLT/DRT work is likely mostly English, while taluk and criminal courts skew Kannada [INFERENCE - no data].
- Implication: document intelligence must handle Kannada script (and mixed Kannada-English, plus legacy non-Unicode Kannada fonts in older scanned records [INFERENCE]), with English HC judgments. Machine translations from HC are labelled translations, not authoritative [INFERENCE].

## Implications for Bengaluru court adapters (inference)

1. Adapter tiers by sanction: (a) user-supplied/uploaded cause list PDFs, emails, or screenshots (zero access risk); (b) official public HTML/PDF (HC cause list, display board, NCLT PDFs) fetched with polite, low-rate access only after written clearance from HC Registry/NIC, or via a lawyer-initiated, in-session assist; (c) captcha-gated status searches (HC case status, judgments, district case status, district cause lists) - treat as human-in-the-loop only; do not bypass captchas.
2. Karnataka HC cause list: model fields as bench -> court hall -> list no. -> serial -> case type/no/year -> stage heading -> party/advocate fields. Heading taxonomy must be an open enum (Preliminary Hearing, Prelim B Group, Hearing on IA, Admission, Final/For Hearing, Being Spoken To, Orders), since official names unverified. Support next-evening posting, bench-specific timing (Bengaluru ~8:36 PM on 16 Oct), and `Last updated` stamps.
3. Court-day view: join cause list to display-board state (CH, list, running Sl. No.) to estimate "likely reach time"; the board has a stated lag so show uncertainty. Stage letters need mapping learned from a lawyer.
4. Advocate identity is searchable by name and Bar Registration Number on the HC dated search; adapter should key a user's own matters by BRN/advocate name aliases (spelling variants, initials) [INFERENCE].
5. Deadline queue: HC advance final-hearing list was discontinued in Nov 2021 and not confirmed restored - assume 1-day notice for final hearings; surface "reached in last list" risk and preparation briefs on a default readiness basis. Early-listing memo mechanism (RJ 187/2023) is a trackable workflow state.
6. Matter graph: keep separate identifiers - HC case no, filing register no, lower-court case no (link), CNR for district matters, NCLT CP/IA numbering with bench code, neutral citation (`YYYY:KHC[-D]:n[-DB]`), blockchain-verified e-judgment as authenticity flag. Case-type table should be data-driven and normalise punctuation (W.P./WP, Crl.P./CRL.P).
7. Bench numbering: Dharwad/Kalaburagi use 100xxx-style numbers [INFERENCE]; do not assume uniform sequences.
8. Language: pipeline needs Kannada OCR/translation, mixed-script tolerance, source-language tagging, and "translation not authoritative" labels; store original. Client updates may need Kannada drafts for trial-court clients.
9. AI-citation risk: Karnataka experience (trial order with non-existent judgments) suggests built-in citation verification against neutral citation/e-judgment search; show verification status in briefs.
10. Terms: footer pages are largely "under construction" and silent on automation; absence is not permission. Draft an authorisation request to HC Registrar (Computers) / NIC and district portal admins; NCLT/DRT similar.
